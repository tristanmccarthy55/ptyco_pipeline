How to use this page

This is the permanent record of the aberration campaign: the state of the work at each checkpoint, the numbers it produced, and the conventions the project runs on. It is written to be handed to a new collaborator — or a new agent session — cold. It is not a meeting deck; the meeting pages are linked in §9 and are rebuilt from the same data.

Read this page first, then the repo documents in this order: aberration_experiment/HANDOFF_ANALYSIS.md (what is waiting right now), HANDOVER.md (the experiment itself), NEXT_PHASE.md (the plan), and PSF_KERNELS.md (the kernel rules). Where this page and those documents disagree, the repo is current and this page is the checkpoint it was written at.

How the project works. The user runs every cluster job — there is no SSH from an agent session. Hand over exact sbatch blocks and wait for the log or the tarball; assume nothing about a job you have not seen finish. Every Blythe path lands under $SHARE/phucrh, never the shared group root. Ship a matching scp -O line with every command block. Local Python is ~/hyperspy-bundle/bin/python. Verify against the code or the data before asserting anything: every wrong number this campaign produced came from an assumption that looked right.
01

The question

A spherical-aberration corrector is specified at an aperture. Tuned for 30 mrad, it nulls third order (C3, "Cs") but leaves fifth order untouched: C5 ≈ 1 mm is a fixed residual of the hardware, not a knob. Open the aperture past its specification and C5θ6 explodes — about 8 radians at the aperture edge at 50 mrad, some 1600 at 120. The wavefront stops being flat, the Ronchigram stops having a sweet spot, and conventional imaging is over.

Ptychography does not need a flat wavefront. It solves for the phase, so an aberrated probe is a known probe, not a broken one — and depth resolution improves as the square of the aperture, δz = λ/α². The question this campaign answers is whether that trade is real: whether an older, cheaper, already-installed instrument, deliberately opened well past its corrector's spec, can deliver depth resolution that a conventional column cannot.

The test object is a 5-cell PbTiO3/SrTiO3 labyrinth slab (19.5 Å of material, 4 Å of vacuum each side, 27.525 Å box, reconstructed in full so surface artefacts land in the vacuum). The readout is atomfind: it models the reconstructed phase volume as a superposition of single-atom responses and locates atoms in three dimensions, blind, then is scored against the known structure. Depth recovery versus aperture is the measurement.

02

The instrument as modelled

The operator's move, as the aperture opens, is to retune what they can: C3 and defocus C1, against a C5 they cannot touch. The planner (campaign/plan_probe.py → round_sweep.tsv) reproduces that move with an explicit objective: a 4 Å probe first, the flattest Ronchigram second. Probe compactness is the criterion because ptychography recovers the phase itself; flatness is a tiebreak, and a diagnostic of where a conventional instrument would have given up.

Up to 70 mrad a 4 Å probe is reachable, so C3 is held at a realistic residual and defocus does the spreading — the traditional recipe. The smallest probe actually reachable there is far smaller than 4 Å (1.21 / 0.83 / 2.22 Å at 30 / 50 / 70 mrad); the probe is deliberately enlarged to the target, not accidentally. From 90 mrad, 4 Å is out of reach: the probe is the smallest it can be and both C3 and C1 are fighting C5.

What d90 means. Probe size is quoted throughout as d90: the diameter of the circle holding 90 % of the probe's intensity. It is an enclosed-intensity measure, not a full width at half maximum — an aberrated probe has a bright core and long tails, and FWHM would flatter it by ignoring exactly the tails that ruin a reconstruction. d50 and d99 are computed alongside and printed, but only one probe-size curve is ever plotted, so a reader is never left asking which one is "the probe". d99 appears in one place only: it sets the detector binning and decides what fits the reconstruction window (§4).

What the round-α campaign found

How to read the table

  • Depth error falls with aperture — 1.20 → 0.56 → 0.37 Å — and sits far below the diffraction depth resolution δz = λ/α² (7.9 → 2.0 Å across the same range), because the finder fits atom centres rather than trying to resolve them.
  • In-plane error is 0.03 Å at 90–100 mrad, while the atoms genuinely move. Columns in this labyrinth wander in-plane by a median 0.24 Å, up to 0.8 Å — the polar displacements the structure exists to show. They are tracked, not averaged away.
  • 50 mrad's species split is not physics. At 3.93 Å slices the AO and BO2 planes merge and the labels swap: confusion 17.7 %, over the health threshold. The recall numbers on that row are label swapping, and every figure marks them as untrustworthy rather than quietly plotting them.
  • False positives are a benign tail: almost all oxygen, never titanium at any aperture, scoring lower on atomfind's quality measure (0.56–0.61 against 0.73–0.84 for true detections), and at 50 mrad over half of them sit in the vacuum bands. Separable by a quality or depth cut; deliberately left in so the numbers are not flattered.
  • 100 mrad trails 90 on oxygen (88 % against 95 %) and survives every correction — it tracks the probe growing from 6.6 to 11 Å.
  • Recall keeps climbing past the point where the Ronchigram stops being flat, which is the headline: the regime that finishes conventional imaging is the regime this method works best in.
  • Matched kernels are worth about 2× in depth error against a kernel measured from the reconstruction itself.
04

Where it stops: 110 and 120 mrad

110 mrad is settled as not reconstructible, and the reason is geometric rather than fundamental. Its probe is 24.5 Å across, which outgrew the original 20 Å scan field — a scan-to-probe ratio of 0.8, leaving no positional diversity for the solve. The leg was re-run with the field widened to 34 Å, the most the 70 Å box allows, at the same position count (40 × 40 = 1600). The result stands: the object is featureless speckle, the in-plane lattice peak sits at 1.5 times background where it needs to be far above it, and the lead kernel diverges.

120 mrad is not even simulable in this geometry: its d99 is about 105 Å, against a 70 Å reconstruction window at BIN = 1. The probe does not fit the box.

Do not cite the older campaign's a110 / a120 depth rows. Their "diamonds" repeat every two depth slices exactly — the odd/even-layer ambiguity of unregularised multislice, drawn bilinearly — and the aberration-free leg fails there too, which C5 cannot explain.
05

Removing the assumptions, one at a time

Everything above is idealised: the focus known exactly, no counting noise, a perfectly round wavefront. Each step below removes one of those and asks what it costs, shown as the reconstructions themselves with the real atom positions drawn on, so the reader can see what happened rather than be told.

  1. An unknown focus solved, by an outer search A ladder of fixed trial probes recovers the focus to 2 Å and costs nothing in the final numbers. Letting the solver fit focus itself does not work, and is closed.
  2. Shot noise as expected 107 and 106 e/Å² are fine, 105 is marginal, 104 is gone.
  3. A non-round probe, known costs nothing Six-fold at up to 0.45 waves reconstructs like the round probe at 70 and 90 mrad, once two engine bugs in how the probe was oriented were fixed. What limits a wider aperture is the probe's size, not the aberrations as such; see 5.3 and §6.

5.1  Focus: the route that works, and the one that does not

In a real experiment the corrector reports its third- and fifth-order terms, but the defocus is never quite where you think. The outer search works. The same data is reconstructed through a ladder of fixed probes that differ only in defocus, and the solver's own mismatch to the data is read as the objective. Its minimum sits on the true focus at both apertures, and the reconstruction made at the fitted focus loses nothing measurable: depth error 0.56 → 0.59 Å at 70 mrad, 0.37 → 0.44 at 90.

The tidier alternative does not work. Letting the solver fit focus as its one free probe parameter, while it fits the object, was built into the engine. At 90 mrad the focus never moves, by less than 1 Å from starts 32 Å out. At 70 mrad it moves but goes the wrong way, ending about 21 Å below the true focus whatever it started from. Freezing the object does not fix it; capping the step does not fix it. The reason is the same degeneracy the right-hand panel above measures: with focus and object both free, the pair slides along the direction the data cannot see.

5.2  Shot noise

Poisson counting noise applied to the same simulated data at four electron doses, so nothing changes but the statistics. It came out as expected, and that is worth saying plainly: it is the one step that behaved. 107 and 106 e/Å² hold; 105 still works but the light atoms start to go; at 104 the single-atom reference could not be measured from the data at all, so the finder was never run. The reconstructions are below the table.

5.3  Non-round aberrations, probe known

A hexapole corrector nulls third-order spherical aberration and leaves six-fold astigmatism behind. No round knob touches it and it grows as the sixth power of the aperture. The question here is narrower than "how much can a microscope tolerate": with the probe known, does a non-round aberration limit the object the reconstruction can reach? Each level is a complete pipeline: its own reconstruction, its own single-atom references measured through the same probe, then the finder run blind and scored against the known structure.

Settled on 2026-09-24: a known non-round probe costs nothing. Six-fold at 0.45 waves reconstructs like the round leg at 70 and at 90 mrad. What the table says

  • Every column matches the round leg to within what one reconstruction varies by. The objects agree layer by layer with the round control at a correlation of 0.98 (70 mrad, 0.45 waves) and 0.99 or better at 90 mrad.
  • The small leftovers are not yet separated from noise: oxygen 71 against 75 % at 0.45 waves and 70 mrad, and oxygen false positives rising 35 → 44 → 49 at 90 mrad. Each is a single solve and the solver reseeds every run; sizing run-to-run spread needs four or more repeats.
  • So the aberrations limit the probe, not the object. What stops a wider aperture is the probe outgrowing the box and the scan field, which is §6.

Two bugs, and how they hid

The engine applies a detector orientation, custom_data_flip = [0,0,1], which transposes the diffraction data as it loads; it was chosen by reconstructing a chiral phantom under all eight orientations, with a round probe. The simulation writes the probe in the same axis order as the data, and nothing transposed the probe to match. A round probe is its own transpose, so every round leg was right and no test could expose it. For six-fold the transpose is a 30° rotation. That was fixed on 2026-09-22 in load_from_p.m.

The fix reached only half the solve. The reconstruction runs two engines, a half-resolution presolve and the full engine, and at the end of the presolve save_to_p.m handed the probe on still transposed; the full engine's loader transposed it again, back to the original error. The probes the "fixed" runs saved are identical to the pre-fix runs' (overlap 1.0000). Fixed on 2026-09-23 (40d28a3): the probe now leaves every engine in the frame it arrived in.

Why the first fix looked like it had half worked. It raised lead recall on every level (the presolve's object was now right and seeded the full engine) but left the final residual where it was (37.8 → 37.8 at 0.1 waves, 71 → 74 at 0.45), because the residual is the full engine's and the full engine had not changed. That was read as a free object absorbing a wrong probe. It was not: once the probe was right in both engines the residual fell from 74.0 to 25.5 and lead recall rose from 56 to 98 %. The rule to judge a probe change on the object stands (the in-solver focus fit in 5.1 lowered its error while the object collapsed), but this episode is not evidence for it. The other suspect at the time, the presolve's half-width crop, was tested and cleared: it crops the detector to ±100 mrad, which keeps the whole probe aperture, and a full-width presolve gave 73.4 against 74.0.

The residual floor, and the diagonal ramp

The round legs sit at a residual of 22.6 at 70 mrad but 5.8 at 90. The cause is in the simulation: it summed 4×4 blocks of detector pixels, a pixel-integrating detector the engine does not model. Computed from the simulator's own exit waves, that alone gives 20.2 at 70 mrad (binning 4) and 5.3 at 90 (binning 2), and it does not grow with six-fold. The blocks also start on the zero-angle pixel, so every pattern was displaced by (binning − 1)/2 fine pixels, which the object absorbs as the diagonal phase ramp analysis has been subtracting (measured 0.129 against 0.135 rad/Å predicted at 70 mrad). From 2026-09-24 the simulation samples the detector at the reconstruction's own points instead; no reconstruction has run on it yet.

In context: the instrument we have been assuming

The campaign models a hexapole-corrected 300 kV column tuned for 30 mrad and opened past it. §5.3 shows that with the probe known the aberrations do not limit the object; they limit the size of the probe, and the probe has to fit the 70 Å simulation box and a scan field of at most ~34 Å. So the question is which terms a real corrector leaves, how large, and how fast they grow as the aperture opens.

Rules that must hold

Every wrong number this campaign produced traced back to breaking one of these. They are not style preferences.

  1. Kernels byte-identical to their lab leg. Everything except the object: slices, box, probe, engine settings, scan window. A kernel measured under different conditions is measuring the difference.
  2. Depth regularisation stays off (REGLAYER=0) on every leg. It symmetrises information between layers — which is to say it low-passes the depth axis, the quantity being measured. Fix the object or the scan, never the operator.
  3. One solver step size for every leg in a comparison, pinned in the driver.
  4. Scan field larger than the probe, scan-to-d90 ≳ 1.5. This is what 110 mrad violated.
  5. Register against the right ground truth, in the right frame: a cache built for the simulated geometry, the in-plane origin derived from the object size, per-atom depth registration for full-box reconstructions.
  6. Geometry-coupled constants derived, not typed. Trims, drop distances and atom limits all follow the box.
  7. Prefer the driver to hand-written sbatch lines — it passes absolute paths and moves stale output aside. A hand-written relative path killed a job once already.
  8. Everything on Blythe lands under $SHARE/phucrh, never the shared group root. After a submission, a listing of the group root should show nothing new.
08

Presenting this work

Figures exist in three layers, and mixing them is what makes a figure unreadable. Diagnostics (analysis/make_ronchigram_fig.py, make_mep_volumes_fig.py, collate_atomfind_depth.py) carry every check needed while working — the figure at the top of this page is one. Meeting figures (analysis/make_meeting_figs.py, make_relaxation_figs.py) are one clean figure per result, with a written explanation beside them rather than crammed into the axes. Manuscript figures (analysis/paper/) are one claim per figure, drawn at final journal size.

Conventions that survived contact

  • No value is ever typed into a figure script. Every number is read from the run outputs — the planner's table, the finder's report, the reconstruction file. This is why the pages, the tables and the data cannot drift apart, and it is worth the extra loader every time.
  • One quantity per axis, and one measure per quantity. Probe size is d90 and is called "probe diameter"; a second curve of the same quantity only invites the question of which one is real.
  • Say when a number cannot be trusted, in the drawing. Hollow markers where the species labels fail their health check; the confusion percentage on the table row. A figure that quietly plots an untrustworthy point is worse than one that omits it.
  • Draw the answer next to the guess. Found atoms as rings over true positions as dots, so misses and spurious detections are visible rather than argued.
  • One colour per species everywhere (Okabe–Ito, colour-blind safe: lead blue, titanium orange, oxygen green). Ordered variables like aperture get ordered greys, so colour keeps meaning species across every figure in the set.
  • Let the data pick the claim. The baseline comparison below was first drawn to show the matched kernel beating peak-picking outright. It does not — on the heavy columns plain peak-picking matches it. The figure was rewritten around what is true: oxygen and precision.
  • Plain language, professor-level audience, no buzzwords, and only claims the user could defend out loud.

The manuscript set

Four figures, one claim each, all generated from the same run outputs and drawn at journal column widths — so they are placed in a manuscript at 100 %, never rescaled.

Publishing pages like this one

Embed figures as base64 data URIs inside the HTML. Publishing them as separate files and referencing them by relative path does not render, even though the files list correctly — this cost a round trip once and will again. Budget the page under 16 MB; base64 inflates by a third. This page is built by aberration_experiment/page/build_page.py from the template beside it, which is why it can be rebuilt from the current figures and republished to the same URL rather than becoming a second page.
