An Older Corrector at 80 mrad

Can a widespread, older hexapole-corrected column still give depth-resolved ptychography if its aperture is opened far past the 30 mrad it was built for, with the probe known? So far, yes — once the scan is kept small and fine. This page covers only what is new since the last one.

01

What is new

The result so far

80 mrad, a 49 Å probe: 100 / 67 / 75 % of Pb / Ti / O, depth error 0.48 Å

The CEOS-approximation column opened to 80 mrad reconstructs about as well as a round, aberration-balanced probe at the same aperture. Six-fold astigmatism reaches 22 waves at the aperture edge there — fifty times the most the six-fold ladder tested at 70 and 90 mrad — and with the probe known it is not what limits. It holds when the corrector is tuned at 30 mrad and left alone, the more realistic case.

The setups, coefficient by coefficient

{{FRAG_EXPLORER}}

Who can change what, on an instrument like the target — a JEOL ARM200F with its 2009 CEOS probe corrector: the operator tunes C1, A1, B2, A2, C3, S3 and A3 in routine use (what the corrector's tuning measures and corrects). Everything else is fixed on the day: C5 is set at the factory, A4 and B4 are parasitic, A5 is intrinsic to the hexapole design, and D4 is not a routine knob. A ± in a measured tableau is measurement uncertainty, not a range anyone can tune over. Sourced: A5 = 1.0 mm (CEOS). Assumed: A4 = B4 = 10 µm, C5 = 1 mm.

The sweep's tuning assumption is optimistic — and at 80 mrad it did not matter. Every CEOS setup above assumes the operator re-tunes A1, A2, S3, A3 and D4 to 0.1 waves at the edge of each new aperture. Measured on a real ARM200F that is out of reach: at 80 mrad it asks for A2 at 1.2 nm, S3 and A3 at 0.02 µm and D4 at 0.30 µm, 20 to 70 times finer than the tuning can even measure (below). The realistic case is to tune at the ~30 mrad the corrector works at and leave it, which at 80 mrad leaves 0.7 waves of A1, 1.9 of A2, 5 each of S3 and A3 and 13 of D4 on top of the 22 of six-fold. That run reconstructs exactly as well: 100 / 67 / 74 % of Pb / Ti / O, depth error 0.47 Å, against 100 / 67 / 75 % and 0.48 Å re-tuned. With the probe known, how finely it was tuned makes no measurable difference here — what matters is that it is known (§8).

03

The whole wavefront, as the aperture opens

The operator keeps the probe as small as possible at each aperture. What has no knob — six-fold astigmatism above all — grows as the aperture to the sixth power, and by 60 mrad it shapes the whole wavefront.

04

The probes that land on the specimen

With every knob used, the probe stays near 4–5 Å to 50 mrad and then grows fast: 12, 17.5, 25, 35 and 49 Å across (d90) at 60, 65, 70, 75 and 80 mrad. Each probe needs a reconstruction window that holds it, from 17.5 Å up to 105 Å.

05

How runs fail, and what fixes them

Large probes (CEOS 70–80 mrad): a coarse scan step fixed Scaling the scanned field with the probe (1.5 × d90) at a fixed 1600 positions coarsens the step to 1–2 Å, and the solve never fits the data. The badly aberrated outer probe carries little usable information, so the step that matters is set by the compact core. A 20 Å field at 0.5 Å — the round runs' own scan — fixes it at 70, 75 and 80 mrad. It is the step, not the field: the 70 mrad run at its original 38 Å field with a 0.59 Å step (4096 positions) reconstructs too (misfit 0.038). Fewer slices do not help.
Compact round probes at 75–80 mrad: the solver's first stage runs away fixed The planner's 4 Å balance (C3 −7 µm, defocus −100 Å at 80 mrad) fails at the depth sampling the aperture calls for: the coarse first stage of the solve diverges from its second iteration, at 18 or 23 slices; at 14 it survives. The same aperture with a different balance (C3 −9 µm, defocus −160 Å, a 5.6 Å probe) converges at 18 slices. What separates them so far: how much the probe's intensity changes from one slice to the next — 6.2 % or more converged, 5.6 % or less failed. A 3.9 Å round 75 balance chosen by that rule before running (C3 −7 µm, defocus −120 Å, 7.2 % per slice) converged: 95 / 75 / 78 %, 0.60 Å.

Results

The CEOS column tracks the round control at every aperture from 50 to 80 mrad. Lead is strong throughout (88–100 % from 60 mrad); titanium is the weak species (58–86 %). At 65 mrad and below, species labels fail the 5 % confusion check for both columns, so the species split there is not to be trusted. At 80 mrad the corrector tuned at 30 mrad and left gives the same numbers as the idealised re-tuning. Each point is a single run.

07

Fixed on the way

The analysis on the cluster read the wrong file fixed Kernel extraction took a solver checkpoint — sometimes the coarse first stage, sometimes an older run — instead of the final reconstruction. It produced "17.5 Å windows fail" and "CEOS 75 fails"; both are withdrawn. From the final reconstructions, every 17.5 Å kernel is clean.
The kernel site finder counted noise as atoms fixed On CEOS 75 noise between grid atoms was picked up as extra sites. Sites must now be the strongest within one grid spacing; clean kernels come out byte-identical.
A large simulation ran out of memory fixed The region simulation held every pattern in double precision; at 5776 positions it was killed. It now holds single precision with bit-identical output. A reconstruction at 1422-pixel patterns needs about four times its data in memory, so about 5500 positions is the limit on one node.
Nothing drew the object added Every analysed run now ships phase images — the depth sum, every slice, and a depth section — including the runs that fail, so a failure is looked at before it is explained.

Next: a real instrument's tableau

  • A true-to-life run — the measured tableau of the ARM200F (§2) at 300 kV: its measured first- to fourth-order residuals, the reference values for B4, D4 and A5, C5 at 4 mm (a well-tuned instrument, inside the measured 6 ± 4 mm), with only defocus and C3 set for the smallest probe.
  • The probe known only as well as the corrector measures it — reconstruct with a probe off by the tableau's own measurement uncertainty. At 80 mrad that uncertainty is several waves per term, so this decides whether "probe known" can be achieved on a real instrument — the question behind this whole sweep.

Still open: at 40 mrad the reconstruction has only four slices and the atom finder cannot run on so thin a kernel; the 40 mrad runs reconstruct, but have no numbers.
