An Older Corrector at 80 mrad

Can a widespread, older hexapole-corrected column still give depth-resolved ptychography if its aperture is opened far past the 30 mrad it was built for, with the probe known? So far, yes — once the scan is kept small and fine. This page covers only what is new since the last one.

01

What is new

The result so far

80 mrad, a 49 Å probe: 100 / 67 / 75 % of Pb / Ti / O, depth error 0.48 Å

The CEOS-approximation column opened to 80 mrad reconstructs about as well as a round, aberration-balanced probe at the same aperture. Six-fold astigmatism reaches 22 waves at the aperture edge there — fifty times the most the six-fold ladder tested at 70 and 90 mrad — and with the probe known it is not what limits.

  • The instrument is a CEOS-approximation tableau. Six-fold A5 = 1 mm (the CEOS figure for hexapole correctors); parasitic A4 = B4 = 10 µm (assumed); every term the operator can tune is retuned at each aperture to 0.1 waves; defocus, C3 and coma are set for the smallest probe.
  • Every run is a 210 Å cut of the tiled crystal, bulk all round, recorded on a point-sampled detector (the old 4 × 4 pixel sum is gone: it set the old residual floors and the diagonal phase ramp).
  • The large probes needed a small, fine scan — §4. The first sweep scaled the scanned field with the probe at fixed position count, which coarsened the step to 1–2 Å; those runs failed.
  • The round controls at 75 and 80 mrad needed a different balance — §6. The planner's 4 Å probes there make the solver diverge at the depth sampling the aperture calls for.
  • Two pipeline faults were found and fixed, and the two conclusions they had produced withdrawn — §7.
02

The instrument, as the aperture opens

The operator keeps the probe as small as possible at each aperture. Everything with a knob is retuned; what has no knob — six-fold astigmatism above all — grows as the aperture to the sixth power. By 60 mrad it is the aberration.

03

The probes that land on the specimen

With every knob used, the probe stays near 4–5 Å to 50 mrad and then grows fast: 12, 17.5, 25, 35 and 49 Å across (d90) at 60, 65, 70, 75 and 80 mrad. Each probe needs a reconstruction window that holds it, from 17.5 Å up to 105 Å.

04

What made the large probes work: the scan

The first sweep grew the scanned field with the probe (1.5 × d90) at a fixed 1600 positions, so the step grew to 0.95, 1.35 and 1.85 Å at 70, 75 and 80 mrad. The 70 and 80 mrad runs never fitted the data; 75 fitted but noisily. The idea behind the fix: the badly aberrated outer probe carries little usable information, so the step that matters is set by the compact core, and 1–2 Å is too coarse for it. Scanning a 20 Å field at 0.5 Å — the round runs' own scan, 1600 positions — both runs reconstruct.

Not yet separated: step or field. The 20 Å run changes both at once. A run at the old 38 Å field with a 0.59 Å step (4096 positions) is reconstructing now and will say which one mattered. Fewer slices do not help: CEOS 70 on the old scan at 10 slices instead of 14 still fails (ε 0.20).
05

Results

From 60 to 75 mrad the CEOS column matches or beats the round control species by species. At 80 mrad it leads on Pb and depth error but trails on Ti (67 against 79 %) and O (75 against 81 %) — single runs, and the engine reseeds every run, so a gap of that size is not yet separated from run-to-run spread. The depth error falls from 0.85 Å at 60 mrad to 0.48 Å at 80 (the 75 mrad point is still on its old scan). At 65 mrad and below, with slices of 2.3 Å and thicker against 1.95 Å between atomic planes, species labels fail the 5 % confusion check for both columns, as in the round campaign.

06

Why the round controls at 75 and 80 mrad changed

The round planner reaches 4 Å at 75 and 80 mrad by balancing C3 and defocus. Those probes failed in every geometry tried. It is not the aperture: the same aperture with a different balance works. It is the probe against the number of depth slices — the solver's first, coarse stage runs away from its second iteration. With a balance that keeps the probe near 4–6 Å but changes more from slice to slice, it converges.

07

Fixed on the way

The analysis on the cluster read the wrong file fixed Kernel extraction took a solver checkpoint — sometimes the coarse first stage, sometimes an older run — instead of the final reconstruction. It produced "17.5 Å windows fail" and "CEOS 75 fails"; both are withdrawn. From the final reconstructions, every 17.5 Å kernel is clean.
The kernel site finder counted noise as atoms fixed On CEOS 75 noise between grid atoms was picked up as extra sites. Sites must now be the strongest within one grid spacing; clean kernels come out byte-identical.
A large simulation ran out of memory fixed The region simulation held every pattern in double precision; at 5776 positions it was killed. It now holds single precision with bit-identical output. A reconstruction at 1422-pixel patterns needs about four times its data in memory, so about 5500 positions is the limit on one node.
Nothing drew the object added Every analysed run now ships phase images — the depth sum, every slice, and a depth section — including the runs that fail, so a failure is looked at before it is explained.

08

Running now

  • Step or field — CEOS 70 at the old 38 Å field with a 0.59 Å step (4096 positions).
  • CEOS 75 on the 20 Å scan — the last large probe still on its old scan.
  • A stable round 75 control — C3 −7 µm, defocus −120 Å: a 3.9 Å probe predicted to converge (fig. 7b).

Still open: at 40 mrad the reconstruction has only four slices and the atom finder cannot run on so thin a kernel; the 40 mrad runs reconstruct, but have no numbers.
