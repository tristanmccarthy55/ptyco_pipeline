ARM200F at 80 mrad

The CEOS page asked whether an older corrector opened far past its design aperture still gives depth-resolved ptychography with the probe known. This page puts in the user's own ARM200F as measured, finds what limits the largest apertures, and then takes the probe away: first a probe known only to a few per cent, then phonons, partial coherence and shot noise on top, and finally a probe found from the data by a search followed by refinement.

Where we are

The result so far

Probe known, 80 mrad: 100 / 67 / 74 % of Pb / Ti / O, depth error 0.45 Å

The measured ARM200F tableau, with the four terms its operator tunes set for the smallest probe, reconstructs from 50 to 80 mrad when the probe is known. Above 80 mrad the probe outgrows the reconstruction window. From realistic data, with phonons, partial coherence and shot noise, a search over defocus and C3 finds both far more precisely than the corrector measures them; whether the probe refined from there brings back the depth information lands on the morning of 9 October.

The findings, in the order the page builds them

  • Probe known. 50–80 mrad reconstruct. Depth error falls from about 0.9 Å at 50–65 mrad to 0.45 Å at 80. Species labels below about 70 mrad were not expected to be reliable, and they are not.
  • The window, not the aperture, stops 90 and 100 mrad. The 80 mrad probe, which works in a 105 Å window, fails in exactly the way 90 and 100 mrad do once its window is shrunk until it loses as much of the probe.
  • A probe 5 % off in C1, C3 and A1 comes back with coherent data, given enough iterations (500) or modes (6): the atoms match the known-probe run; false detections stay higher.
  • With phonons, partial coherence and shot noise, depth is lost whether the true probe is held fixed or a kicked probe is updated. Ten times the dose changes nothing.
  • An outer search finds the probe from that realistic data: a grid of fixed-probe trials over C1 and C3 lands within 2.3 Å and 0.14 µm of the truth at both doses.

03

Probe known, 40 to 100 mrad

With the probe handed to the reconstruction exactly, the ARM200F tableau reconstructs from 50 to 80 mrad. At 80 mrad it matches the CEOS-approximation result of the last page. Below 70 mrad the depth sections still show bars rather than separate atoms, as expected from the depth of field (λ/α² is 5.5 Å at 60 mrad), and the species labels are not reliable there. 90 mrad degrades on one side of the field and 100 mrad loses the depth information; §4 shows why.

04

What stops 90 and 100 mrad: the window

Both large apertures fail with the probe known, so the probe fit cannot be the cause. What changes is how much of the probe falls outside the 105 Å window the reconstruction works in: 3.3 % at 80 mrad, 13.6 % at 90 and 23 % at 100. To test that alone, the 80 mrad probe was reconstructed in smaller windows, with nothing else changed.

{{FRAG_WINDOW_TABLE}}
Rule from this test: keep the share of the probe outside the window below about 5 %. 90 mrad needs a 140 Å window (5.1 % outside), which fits in a 280 Å region at about 1540 px patterns: that is the final run (§8). 100 mrad needs 210–280 Å (7.3 % and 1.8 %), 2560–3400 px patterns, past the 48 h limit and the node's memory today.
05

A probe known to 5 %: coherent data

The reconstruction now starts from the tableau with C1, C3 and A1 off by 5 % and is left to refine the probe. A 5 % error is a wave or more at 80 mrad, and about five at 100: the table gives it per aperture. With coherent data the probe comes back at 40 mrad, mostly at 60 (the object slides in depth to absorb part of the defocus error, the trade-off the C1 search found earlier), and at 80 mrad only partly within 200 iterations. Given 500 iterations, or 6 modes, the 80 mrad atoms match the known-probe run, with more false detections.

06

Realistic physics: phonons, partial coherence, shot noise

The 80 mrad data are now simulated with 16 frozen-phonon configurations, a focal spread and source size of a cold field emitter, and Poisson noise at 1e7 or 1e8 e/Å². Held fixed, the true coherent probe can no longer describe what the detector sees: the reconstruction puts what it cannot explain into the vacuum layers, and each column comes out as one smeared blob in depth. In plane the lattice is still clean. Ten times the dose changes nothing, so the counting noise is not the limit. A kicked probe released into the same data does not move towards the truth (overlap 0.24 → 0.22 in 200 iterations, 0.26 in 500).

07

Finding the probe from the data

A probe update alone does not recover a 5 % error at 80 mrad from realistic data. An experimenter would first narrow the probe down using the data themselves, then refine it. That is what this run does:

  1. Search.Reconstruct the same lab data 49 times, each time with a fixed trial probe whose C1 and C3 are 0.91–1.09 times the kicked values (3 % steps), 50 iterations each.
  2. Choose.Take the trial with the lowest final residual and refine its position with a quadratic fit to the 3 × 3 trials around it.
  3. Refine.Release the probe from that start, with 4 modes, for 500 iterations; the Pb and Ti grids that make the atom-finding kernels start from the same answer.

08

The final run: 90 mrad in a 140 Å window

The best shot before calling the large-aperture question: 90 mrad (depth of field λ/α² = 2.4 Å, against 3.1 Å at 80) in the largest window the cluster can reconstruct, 140 Å, losing 5.1 % of the probe. The specimen region grows to 280 Å, so that the simulation's own periodic box holds the probe, and the detector is cut to ±108 mrad (1.2 times the aperture) to keep the patterns near 1540 px. Phonons, partial coherence and 1e8 e/Å², a true-probe start with 4 modes and probe update, and fixed-probe twins whose grids give kernels even if the refined grids fail.

Pending, about 2.5–3 days. The 280 Å simulations first ran out of GPU memory; the grid and batch size were fixed and they are running (about 8 h each), then the reconstructions (about 30 h).
