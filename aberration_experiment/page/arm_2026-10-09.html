Findings

  • Probe known: at 70–80 mrad, 90 % of Pb and about half the Ti are found in the right place; guessing the depth would place about a quarter.
  • 90 and 100 mrad fail because the probe outgrows the window, not because of the aperture.
  • Coherent data: a 5 % probe error comes back with 500 iterations or 6 modes.
  • Phonons, partial coherence, shot noise: depth is lost. The finder still finds the columns, but its depths are barely better than guessing (Pb 23 % against 16–19 %). Ten times the dose changes nothing.
  • Outer search: finds C1 and C3 from the realistic data to within 2.3 Å and 0.14 µm.

03

Probe known, 40 to 100 mrad

Coherent data (no phonons, energy spread or noise) and the probe known exactly: 50–80 mrad reconstruct, and 80 mrad matches the CEOS result. Below 70 mrad the depth sections still show bars (λ/α² is 5.5 Å at 60 mrad). 90 and 100 mrad fail for the reason in §4.

04

What stops 90 and 100 mrad: the window

Both fail with the probe known. What changes is the share of the probe outside the 105 Å window: 3.3 % at 80 mrad, 13.6 % at 90, 23 % at 100. Shrinking the window on the 80 mrad probe alone reproduces the failure.

{{FRAG_WINDOW_TABLE}}
Rule: keep under about 5 % of the probe outside the window. 90 mrad needs 140 Å (the final run, §8); 100 mrad needs 210–280 Å, beyond the 48 h limit and the node's memory today.
05

A probe off by 5 %, coherent data

Coherent data as in §3; the reconstruction starts with C1, C3 and A1 5 % off and refines the probe: about a wave of error at 80 mrad, five at 100. At 80 mrad the atoms come back with 500 iterations or 6 modes, with more wrong elements than the known probe. This start is harder than an experimenter's: the search in §7 corrects C1 and C3, and A1's 5 % is only 0.04 waves.

06

Realistic physics: phonons, partial coherence, shot noise

The same 80 mrad probe with phonons, the cold FEG's energy spread and source size, and shot noise (values in §B). With the true coherent probe held fixed, the reconstruction puts what it cannot explain into the vacuum layers and each column smears in depth. The finder still finds the columns, but its depths are barely better than guessing, and its own 95 % bars widen to about ±1.9 Å. Ten times the dose changes nothing. A kicked probe released into the same data does not move towards the truth (overlap 0.24 → 0.26 in 500 iterations).

07

Finding the probe from the data

A probe update alone does not recover the 5 % error from realistic data, so the probe is first found from the data:

  1. Search.49 fixed-probe trials, C1 and C3 at 0.91–1.09 × the kicked values, 50 iterations each.
  2. Choose.The lowest final residual, refined by a quadratic fit over the 3 × 3 trials around it.
  3. Refine.Release the probe from there with 4 modes for 500 iterations.
