01

The specification

The point of the exercise is a number an instrument scientist can act on. A hexapole corrector nulls third-order spherical aberration and leaves six-fold astigmatism behind: round knobs cannot touch it, and it grows as the sixth power of the aperture. The question is how much of it the method tolerates.

At 70 mrad, known probe, no other imperfection

Six-fold astigmatism must stay below
0.1 waves at the aperture edge — C56 < 10 µm

And that is an upper bound, not the tolerance: 0.1 waves is the smallest residual tested and it already fails. The true limit lies below it and this round did not bracket it.

0.1 wavessmallest residual tested
98 → 80 %lead found, there
1.3 → 12.4 %species confusion, against a 5 % health threshold
≈ 1 mmwhat a corrector actually leaves

The last two figures are the uncomfortable part. A published hexapole tableau leaves C56 of order 1 mm, which at 70 mrad is about ten waves. The requirement is therefore roughly a hundred times better than as-left, and it tightens as the aperture opens: the same 0.1 waves is C56 < 10 µm at 70 mrad but < 2.2 µm at 90 mrad, because one wave costs 1.00 × 106 Å at 70 and 2.22 × 105 Å at 90.

What this does not yet say. It is one term, at one aperture, with everything else idealised. Six-fold is the corrector's signature residual, but it is not the only non-round term that reaches waves at these apertures — the fourth-order three-lobe term is comparably large in a real tableau — and the tolerance per wave should be expected to depend on the order, because a term of order n throws the edge rays by (n+1)λ/α. That cross-check is §7, and it is the next thing to run.

The threshold, rung by rung

Each rung is a complete pipeline: the labyrinth reconstructed through a probe carrying that much six-fold astigmatism, its own lead and titanium reference grids reconstructed through the same probe to measure the matched kernel, then the atom finder run blind and scored against the known structure. The criterion was fixed before the numbers were read: keep at least 90 % of the round probe's lead recall and hold depth error under 0.7 Å.

Six-fold astigmatism at 70 mrad, known probe, no counting noise, static atoms. Recall is over interior planes. Confusion is the atom finder's own species-labelling health check, whose threshold is 5 %. Kernel peak-to-background is measured from that rung's own reference grids, so it reports the reconstruction rather than the finder. How to read the table

  • No rung passes. The first one tested, 0.10 waves, keeps 82 % of the round probe's lead recall where the criterion asked for 90 %, and its depth error is 0.76 Å against a 0.7 Å limit. The tolerance is below the bottom of the ladder.
  • Species labelling fails before recall does. Confusion crosses its 5 % health threshold at the first rung and is at 28 % by the second. Where it has failed, the split between lead, titanium and oxygen is label swapping and should not be read as physics — which is why those rows carry the chip rather than being quietly plotted.
  • Depth error is the wrong thing to watch here. It only creeps from 0.56 to 1.03 Å across the whole ladder, because it is measured on the atoms still being found — and by 1.2 waves that is 7 % of them. Recall is the honest indicator; depth error looks reassuring precisely because the difficult atoms have dropped out of it.
  • Above about 0.6 waves the numbers stop being ordered. 2.50 waves scores higher than 1.20 on lead. Both are far past failure and the differences there are the scatter of a handful of surviving detections, not a trend.
  • In-plane error grows fivefold, 0.04 → 0.22 Å, and that one is real: it crosses the 0.24 Å median displacement the polar structure itself carries, so the measurement stops being able to see the physics it exists to measure.
04

Is it the reconstruction, or the finder?

A fair objection: the finder's quality cut was tuned on clean wavefronts, so a collapse in recall might be the threshold discarding good atoms rather than the reconstruction losing them. The test costs no cluster time — re-run the blind finder on the same reconstructions with the cut relaxed from 0.50 to 0.35, and include the round baseline as the control.

Both, and the control separates them. On the round baseline the relaxed cut changes recall by four points of oxygen and nothing else, while precision falls from 0.98 to 0.81 — it is buying false positives, not finding hidden atoms. So the large recoveries on the aberrated rungs are real: at 0.10 waves the finder had been discarding genuine atoms whose match to a degraded kernel had dropped below the cut, and lead comes back from 80 to 89 %, oxygen from 48 to 69 %.

That does not rescue the specification. Read at the generous threshold, 0.10 waves still costs nine points of lead against the baseline's 98 %, ten points of oxygen, and half again the depth error (0.58 → 0.86 Å); and species confusion is 8.7 %, still past its 5 % health threshold. The conclusion holds with the finder tuned in the aberrated legs' favour.

The same test on thermal vibration comes back empty. Relaxing the identical cut on the frozen-phonon legs changes their numbers by nothing at all — 71 / 35 / 10 % at 70 mrad and 59 / 39 / 17 % at 90, before and after, to two decimal places. So the phonon collapse recorded on the ladder is the reconstruction and the physics, not the finder's tuning. That closes an open question the previous round left, and it is what makes the non-round recovery above believable rather than an artefact of turning a knob.
05

Fitting focus inside the solver: closed

The working way to recover an unknown focus is an outer search: reconstruct the same data through a ladder of fixed trial probes that differ only in defocus, and take the minimum. It recovers focus to 2 Å and costs essentially nothing in the final numbers. The tidier alternative — let the solver fit focus as its one free probe parameter, while it fits the object — was built into the engine and failed. This round establishes why, and closes it.

The first explanation, overshoot from an oversized step, was already disproved by the step statistics. The live hypothesis was the degeneracy the fixed-probe scan had measured directly: probe focus and object depth trade one for one, so with both free the pair slides along a direction the data cannot see. Experiment A tests exactly that by freezing the object in the full-resolution engine; A2 caps the focus step at 0.05 Å per iteration.

  • At 90 mrad the focus does not move at all. From starts 16 and 32 Å away, with the object free or frozen, the cumulative shift never reaches 1 Å. The one-parameter gradient carries no usable signal at that aperture, and freezing the object does not create one.
  • At 70 mrad it moves, but never toward the truth. With the object free, every run that moves ends near 21 Å below the true focus whatever it started from. Freezing the object changes the direction rather than fixing it: started at the truth the fit wanders to +8 Å, started 6 Å above it runs to +27 Å.
  • The step cap is not the fix. At 0.05 Å per iteration, started at the true focus, the fit still walks 18.5 Å away. Overshoot is ruled out for good.
  • The machinery is sound, which is what makes the negative result trustworthy: the focus measured independently from the refined probe agrees with the engine's own reported shift on every run.
What this changes. Nothing about the method as practised, and that is the point — the outer search over trial probes is not a stopgap, it is the answer. Say so plainly rather than leaving the in-solver route open as unfinished business. One caveat to keep: freezing the object degrades the 70 mrad reconstruction on its own, because the object is then never refined at full resolution, so the 70 mrad arm of experiment A is a weaker test than the 90 mrad arm. The 90 mrad result needs no such qualification.

07

What is left, and what each piece needs

In the order that matters. The first is cheap and follows directly from this round; the last is the one that makes the result publishable.

  1. Bracket the tolerance, and test whether it transfers across orders. A finer six-fold ladder below 0.1 waves, and one term per order at the same wave level — two-fold, three-fold and the fourth-order three-lobe term, which a real tableau leaves as large as six-fold. Both run through the existing pipeline with no new simulation code, because the aberration tableau already reaches the simulation as a dictionary. The deliverable is a specification per term, Cnm < (n+1)·Z·λ/αn+1, with Z measured per order rather than assumed.
  2. Read out the 70 Å sample when it lands. Thickness is the regime the method exists for. The analysis path is built and checked: the ground truth for the 18-cell slab reproduces the simulated box to better than 0.01 Å, and the depth constants are now derived from the box rather than typed. Its comparison must be against a thin slab at the same solver step, or the result is about the step size and not about thickness.
  3. Partial coherence. New simulation code: an incoherent sum over a Gaussian spread of focus, about 15 Å at one standard deviation for a cold field emitter at 300 kV, then over source position. The reconstruction side can already absorb it through probe modes.
  4. Specimen tilt of a few degrees, validated on the thin slab first — on the 70 Å slab the shear passes the 1.95 Å column spacing at about 2°, and the column picture itself breaks down there.
  5. The combined run: every surviving relaxation switched on together, across apertures, with matched kernels and the same figure scripts.
One thing worth asking for. The realistic-tableau leg is currently built from published order-of-magnitude residuals. A measured aberration tableau from the instrument this work is aimed at would replace them, and it is the leg a referee would trust.
