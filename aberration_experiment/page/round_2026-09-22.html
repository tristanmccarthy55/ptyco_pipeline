The Six-Fold Tolerance

Three idealisations removed from the depth-resolved ptychography experiment, one at a time: an unknown focus, a finite electron dose, and a corrector residual that round knobs cannot fix. Shown as the reconstructions themselves, with the real atom positions drawn on.

01

What we did

The experiment reconstructs a thin lead-titanate slab by multislice ptychography at 70 mrad and then finds its atoms in three dimensions, blind. With everything idealised it recovers 98 % of the lead to 0.56 Å in depth. Each step below removes one idealisation and asks what it costs.

  1. Let the solver fit the focus does not work Twenty-two runs. None of them recover the focus. The outer search over trial probes, which does work, stays the method.
  2. Turn the electron dose down as expected 107 and 106 e/Å² are fine, 105 is marginal, 104 is gone entirely.
  3. Add six-fold astigmatism very tight limit The corrector's own residual. Even the smallest amount tested hurts, so the tolerance is below the bottom of the ladder.

An upper bound, not the tolerance: 0.1 waves is the smallest residual we tested and it already costs a fifth of the lead. A real corrector leaves about 1 mm, roughly ten waves, so this asks for something like a hundred times better than as-left.

02

First: was the aberration actually in the reconstruction?

Worth checking before believing any of it. The simulation builds an aberrated probe, writes it to a file, and the reconstruction is handed that file with the probe held fixed. So the test is direct: rebuild the probe independently from the aberration tableau, and compare it with the probe stored inside the finished reconstruction.

They match to six decimal places. Overlap 1.000000 on all four six-fold legs. The aberration in the reconstruction is exactly the one we asked for.

One thing to know if you repeat the check. The reconstruction file stores the probe twice, and the second copy (p/probe_initial) is written transposed relative to the one the solver used (reconstruction/probes). For a round probe that makes no difference, because a round probe is its own transpose. For a six-fold probe it is not, and comparing the wrong one makes a perfectly good probe look progressively wrong as the aberration grows — 0.95, 0.82, 0.69, 0.57 down the ladder. That is an artefact of reading the wrong field, and nothing else.

03

1 · Letting the solver fit the focus

In a real experiment the corrector tells you its third- and fifth-order terms, but the defocus is never quite where you think. The tidy fix is to let the solver fit focus as its one free probe parameter while it fits the object. We built that into the engine and it does not work.

  • At 90 mrad the focus never moves, by less than 1 Å, from starts as far as 32 Å out. There is no usable gradient.
  • At 70 mrad it moves but goes the wrong way, ending about 21 Å below the true focus regardless of where it started.
  • Freezing the object does not fix it, and capping the step does not fix it. From the true focus the capped run still walks 18.5 Å away, so it is not overshoot.

The reason is a degeneracy we had already measured: probe focus and object depth trade one for one, so with both free the pair slides along a direction the data cannot see. The outer search over trial probes is the answer, and it costs almost nothing in the final numbers.

04

2 · Turning the dose down

Poisson noise applied to the same simulated data, so nothing changes but the counting statistics. This came out as expected, which is worth saying plainly: it is the one step that behaved.

107 and 106 e/Å² hold up. 105 still works but the columns are visibly noisier and the light atoms start to go. At 104 there is nothing left — and the failure is not that the finder missed atoms, but that the single-atom reference could not be measured from that data at all, so the finder was never run.

05

3 · Adding six-fold astigmatism

This is the one that matters, because it is what a hexapole corrector leaves behind and what no round knob can remove. Four levels were run at 70 mrad, each a complete pipeline: its own reconstruction, its own single-atom reference measured through the same probe, then the finder run blind and scored.

There is no passing row. The tolerance is below 0.1 waves and this round did not reach it, so the next runs bracket it from underneath at 0.02, 0.04 and 0.07 waves.

Don't read the depth error as good news. It only creeps from 0.56 to 0.86 Å, because it is measured on the atoms that were still found — and by the bottom row that is a third of the lead. Recall is the honest column here.
06

Whose fault is it: the reconstruction, or the finder?

A fair objection, and one you can raise just by looking at the pictures: there are clearly still peaks in those panels, and plenty of them carry no ring. So we tested it two ways.

By eye, below: the same slice bare, then with the true positions, then with the detections. By experiment, we re-ran the finder on the identical reconstructions with its quality threshold relaxed, and included the clean baseline as a control.

  • On the clean baseline the relaxed threshold changes recall by almost nothing. So it does not simply invent atoms.
  • On the 0.1-wave leg it brings lead back from 80 to 89 % and oxygen from 48 to 69 %. So the finder had been throwing away real atoms whose match to a degraded reference had fallen below the cut. Part of the collapse is ours to fix.
  • But not all of it: even read that generously, 0.1 waves still costs nine points of lead and half again the depth error, and the species labels have already failed their health check.
  • On the thermal-vibration legs the same change does nothing whatsoever. That collapse is real physics, not tuning.
07

What happens next

  1. Bracket the tolerance with six-fold at 0.02, 0.04 and 0.07 waves.
  2. Check whether the limit transfers to other aberrations. Six-fold is not the only non-round term that reaches waves at this aperture; the fourth-order three-lobe term is just as large in a real corrector tableau. Two-fold, three-fold and three-lobe go in at the same wave levels. Both sets of rows are written and the probes check out.
  3. The thick sample, 70 Å instead of 20, is on the cluster now.
  4. Partial coherence and specimen tilt still need simulation code.
  5. Then everything at once, which is the run that makes this publishable.
