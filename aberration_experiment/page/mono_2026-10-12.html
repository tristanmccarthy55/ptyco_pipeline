Parameters and method

microscope300 kV, 80 mrad aperture, the ARM200F's measured aberrations (defocus, C3, B2 and A1 retuned for 80 mrad)
specimen, scanPbTiO3/SrTiO3, 5 unit cells (19.5 Å) with 4 Å of vacuum each side; 20 Å scanned in 0.5 Å steps; detector to 133 mrad
knob 1: focal spreadThe gun's energy spread ΔE passed through the objective lens's chromatic aberration Cc: electrons of different energy focus at different heights, a spread of defocus of 17.4 Å rms per eV·mm at 300 kV. We scan it in Å, so the result holds for any ΔE and Cc.
knob 2: source sizeThe gun's finite emitting area, demagnified onto the specimen: each electron's probe lands slightly shifted. Given as the FWHM of that spread of positions, in Å.
how it is simulated16 copies of the scan, each with its own defocus offset and probe shift drawn from these spreads; the diffraction patterns are averaged. No phonons, no shot noise.
reconstructionmultislice ptychography, 18 slices; the true probe held fixed, or released with 7–8 probe modes (step 2B)
how it is judgedthe share of Pb and Ti atoms the finder places as the right element within 0.5 Å of their true depth. Guessing the depths along each column gives about 26 % for Pb. good Pb ≥ 80 % and Ti ≥ 45 % · okay Pb ≥ 50 % · off otherwise

What we did

{{FRAG_STATUS}}

What we found

  • Focal spread: fine up to 3.5 Å, gone by 5 Å. It fails by loading the vacuum layers above and below the slab.
  • Source size: fine up to 0.1 Å, Ti lost at 0.2 Å, gone by 0.4 Å. It fails differently: the columns smear in depth while the vacuum stays clean.
  • Together they compound: 2.5 Å with a 0.2 Å source is off, though each passed alone.
  • Probe modes help: at 7.29 Å the result goes from off to okay (Pb 31 → 70 %); Ti is still short of good.
  • For instruments: good needs ΔE ≤ 0.14 eV at Cc 1.4 mm (≤ 0.08 eV at 2.4 mm). The Spectra 300 monochromators get there, a Titan's Wien filter only at its best. Real sources, 0.24–0.6 Å, are the harder limit.

What is next

  1. Finish the probe-mode pointsat 5 Å and at the 0.2 and 0.4 Å sources, rerunning now.
  2. Fix the solver's step rule for many modes(it sets the step from the strongest mode alone; three runs crash on it).
  3. Test a realistic sourceof 0.2–0.3 Å with probe modes. To decide: which focal spreads.
  4. Phase 2: temperature(phonons at room temperature, 100 K and colder) at the settings that survive.
  5. Get a Spectra 300's Cc, ripple and source sizeto place it on the wall as a point.
02

Focal spread alone

Up to 3.5 Å the atoms stay in place. From 5 Å the vacuum layers are nearly as strong as the slab and each column becomes one leaning streak: the columns are found, their depths are not.

03

Source size alone

The vacuum stays clean while the columns lose their atom-by-atom contrast. Ti goes first. The simulated source is the kind case: at 0.2 Å, 9 modes hold 99 % of its beam, where a real source needs about 17.

04

Both at once

The losses compound. A 0.1 Å source leaves 2.5 Å good but drops 3.5 Å to okay; a 0.2 Å source puts both off. Together they fail like the source alone, plus the focal spread's vacuum loading at 3.5 Å.

05

Probe modes

With the probe fixed, the reconstruction can put the partial coherence nowhere but the object. Here the probe is released with 7–8 modes, started from the mixed state of a real gun with each setting.

7.29 Å: off → okay. Pb 70 % (fixed probe 31 %), the vacuum loading halves and separate Pb sites return; Ti 32 % is short of good. The state stays mixed (fig_modes). The 0.2 Å source run looks cleaner than its control but has no atoms yet.
Caveat for every mode run. The solver sets its step from the strongest mode alone and divides the object step by the number of modes. With a real mixed state three runs crash on it, twice; they are rerunning with larger groups.
