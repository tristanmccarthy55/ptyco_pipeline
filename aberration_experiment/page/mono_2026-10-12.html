Depth survives, probe known

{{WALL_LINE}}

Each effect alone leaves some margin; together they compound; probe modes win part of it back. At the Spectra Mono's best setting the depth survives with a known probe, and cooling to 20 K brings back the Pb layers but not Ti.

{{FRAG_HEAD_GRID}}

Parameters and method

microscope300 kV, 80 mrad aperture, the ARM200F's measured aberrations (defocus, C3, B2 and A1 retuned for 80 mrad)
specimen, scanPbTiO3/SrTiO3, 5 unit cells (19.5 Å) with 4 Å of vacuum each side; 20 Å scanned in 0.5 Å steps; detector to 133 mrad
knob 1: focal spreadThe gun's energy spread ΔE passed through the objective lens's chromatic aberration Cc: electrons of different energy focus at different heights, a spread of defocus of 17.4 Å rms per eV·mm at 300 kV. We scan it in Å, so the result holds for any ΔE and Cc.
knob 2: source sizeThe gun's finite emitting area, demagnified onto the specimen: each electron's probe lands slightly shifted. Given as the FWHM of that spread of positions, in Å.
how it is simulated16 copies of the scan, each with its own defocus offset and probe shift drawn from these spreads; the diffraction patterns are averaged. No phonons, no shot noise.
reconstructionmultislice ptychography, 18 slices; the true probe held fixed, or released with 7–8 probe modes (step 2B)
how it is judgedthe share of Pb and Ti atoms the finder places as the right element within 0.5 Å of their true depth. Guessing the depths along each column gives about 26 % for Pb. good Pb ≥ 80 % and Ti ≥ 45 % · okay Pb ≥ 50 % · off otherwise

What we did

{{FRAG_STATUS}}

What we found

  • Focal spread: fine up to 3.5 Å, gone by 5 Å. It fails by loading the vacuum layers above and below the slab.
  • Source size: fine up to 0.1 Å, Ti lost at 0.2 Å, gone by 0.4 Å. It fails differently: the columns smear in depth while the vacuum stays clean.
  • Together they compound: 2.5 Å with a 0.2 Å source is off, though each passed alone.
  • Probe modes help: at 7.29 Å the result goes from off to okay (Pb 31 → 70 %); Ti is still short of good. The crashes of three mode runs were a solver bug, now fixed (§05); all four points are rerunning.
  • At the Spectra Mono point (2.5 Å, about 90 meV): good with a 0.1 Å source and probe modes, okay with a realistic 0.2 Å source, where the fixed probe failed (§06).
  • Cooling: with phonons the fixed probe loses the depth even at 20 K; probe modes bring Pb back to okay (51 % at 100 K, 70 % at 20 K) but not Ti, which stays at the guessing level (§06).
  • Finding the probe: an outer search from the corrector's own precision finds A1 to 4 Å but its first C1 answer was 32 Å off, enough to lose the depth; a third search stage is running (§06).
  • For instruments: good needs ΔE ≤ 0.14 eV at Cc 1.4 mm (≤ 0.08 eV at 2.4 mm). The Spectra 300 monochromators get there, a Titan's Wien filter only at its best. Real sources, 0.24–0.6 Å, are the harder limit.

What is next

  1. Finishthe four probe-mode points with the corrected solver, and the searched start with its third stage (both running, about 1.5 days).
  2. D: shot noisea dose series, 1e5–1e8 e/Å², with the searched start at 2.5 Å + 0.1 Å + 20 K: the lowest dose that keeps the depth sets the scan time, to weigh against a helium holder's drift.
  3. Ti in depthlost with phonons even at 20 K: is it the method (iterations, kernels) or the physics?
  4. Get a Spectra 300's Cc, ripple, source size and measured aberrationsto place it on the wall as a point rather than a range.

Focal spread alone

Up to 3.5 Å the atoms stay in place. From 5 Å the vacuum layers are nearly as strong as the slab and each column becomes one leaning streak: the columns are found, their depths are not.

Source size alone

The vacuum stays clean while the columns lose their atom-by-atom contrast. Ti goes first. The simulated source is the kind case: at 0.2 Å, 9 modes hold 99 % of its beam, where a real source needs about 17.

Both at once

The losses compound. A 0.1 Å source leaves 2.5 Å good but drops 3.5 Å to okay; a 0.2 Å source puts both off. Together they fail like the source alone, plus the focal spread's vacuum loading at 3.5 Å.

05

Probe modes

With the probe fixed, the reconstruction can put the partial coherence nowhere but the object. Here the probe is released with 7–8 modes, started from the mixed state of a real gun with each setting.

7.29 Å: off → okay. Pb 70 % (fixed probe 31 %), the vacuum loading halves and separate Pb sites return; Ti 32 % is short of good. The state stays mixed (fig_modes). The 0.2 Å source run looks cleaner than its control but has no atoms yet.
Solver bug, found and fixed (2026-10-16). Three mode runs crashed at their start, at every grouping tried. The solver divided the object update by the illumination of the strongest mode alone while the update adds every mode, so with a real mixed state (strongest mode 9–43 %) its steps were 2–11 times too large. It now sums all the modes; a one-mode run is unchanged. All four points are rerunning with the fix; until they land this section shows the earlier runs, marked "earlier solver". The solver still divides the object step by the number of modes, so mode runs converge the object more slowly than fixed-probe runs.

06

The Spectra Mono point: cooling, and finding the probe

The instrument's best realistic setting: 2.5 Å of focal spread (about 90 meV through Cc ≈ 1.6 mm) with a 0.1 or a 0.2 Å source, run three ways: the probe known (A), cooled with phonons at 100 K and 20 K (B), and the probe found from the data rather than given (C).

A, coherence only: good with a 0.1 Å source, okay with 0.2 Å. Probe modes take the 0.2 Å point from off to okay (Pb 45 → 55 %). At 0.1 Å the mode run sits a little below its fixed-probe control (Pb 81 vs 91 %, Ti 47 vs 60 %), most likely the solver's divided object step rather than the physics.
B, cooled: Pb comes back, Ti does not. With phonons the fixed probe loses the depth at both temperatures. Probe modes bring Pb back to okay, 51 % at 100 K and 70 % at 20 K; Ti stays at the guessing level even at 20 K, where the atoms still move by their zero-point motion (about 0.04 Å).

C: finding the probe

In an experiment the probe is not given. The corrector reports C1, C3 and A1 with 95 % intervals; the start's errors were drawn from those intervals and an outer search had to find them, before refining with probe modes. Higher orders are held at their true values.

The first search failed on its order, not its method. It searched C1 × C3 while A1 was still about 40 Å wrong, and its C1 answer slid to the edge of the grid, 32 Å off (about 5 waves at the aperture edge). It then found A1 to within 4 Å regardless. With C1 that far off the object moved into the vacuum and the depth was lost. A1 is robust to a C1 error; C1 is not to an A1 error. A third stage, C1 × C3 again at the found A1, is running.
