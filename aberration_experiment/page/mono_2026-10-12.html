The Coherence Wall

Can a widely installed 300 kV monochromated microscope, the Spectra 300 X-FEG Mono, find the depth of each atom along the beam by multislice ptychography? We test what could stop it: a beam that is not perfectly coherent, the atoms' thermal vibration, and not knowing the probe exactly.

What this page is

the questionDoes depth-resolved multislice ptychography still work on a Spectra 300 with the X-FEG Mono, a common instrument, not a one-off? "Works" means the reconstruction puts each Pb and Ti atom at its true depth along the beam.
the testSimulated 4D-STEM data of a thin PbTiO3/SrTiO3 slab at 300 kV and 80 mrad. We add one imperfection at a time, reconstruct, and let an atom finder read each atom's depth from the reconstructed volume.
the scoreThe share of Pb and Ti atoms put at the right depth, within 0.5 Å. Pure guessing scores about 26 % for Pb and 18 % for Ti. good Pb ≥ 80 % and Ti ≥ 45 % · okay Pb ≥ 50 % · off below that.
the aberrationsThe JEOL ARM200F's measured set, retuned for 80 mrad, in every run. We found no measured Spectra set, and the ARM's corrector leaves A5 uncorrected, so this is a pessimistic stand-in.
the order§02–§04: the beam's coherence, with the true probe given. §05: letting the reconstruction refine the beam as several modes. §06: the Spectra Mono's own setting, then cooled, then with the probe found from the data. §07: which microscopes reach the limits found. §08: the claims, and how well each is supported.
The limits found, with the true probe given: the depth survives up to

{{WALL_LINE}}

In one line: with the probe known, the Spectra Mono's setting keeps both Pb and Ti depths if the source is small (0.1 Å); with atomic vibration, even cooled to 20 K, only Pb's. A probe found from the data, and shot noise, are the next two results.

{{FRAG_HEAD_GRID}}

What we found, one limiting factor at a time

  • 1. Energy spread (seen as a spread of focus): the depth survives up to 3.5 Å rms of focal spread and is lost by 5 Å. For good that needs ΔE ≤ 0.14 eV at Cc 1.4 mm (≤ 0.08 eV at 2.4 mm). The Spectra Mono, at about 90 meV, gives 2.5 Å: inside. When it fails, false structure fills the vacuum above and below the specimen (§02).
  • 2. Source size: good up to 0.1 Å, Ti lost at 0.2 Å, off by 0.4 Å. Real effective sources are quoted at 0.24–0.6 Å, so this is the harder limit. When it fails, each column smears in depth while the vacuum stays clean (§03).
  • 3. Both together: worse than either alone. 2.5 Å with a 0.2 Å source is off, though each passed alone (§04).
  • 4. Probe modes, letting the reconstruction describe the beam as a mixture of 7–8 probes instead of one held fixed, win part of it back: at 7.29 Å off → okay, and at the Spectra Mono setting with a 0.2 Å source off → okay. A bug in our solver crashed three of these runs; it is fixed and the single points are rerunning (§05).
  • 5. Atomic vibration: at room temperature the depth is lost. Cooled, with probe modes, Pb comes back to okay (51 % at 100 K, 70 % at 20 K); Ti stays at the guessing level even at 20 K (§06).
  • 6. Not knowing the probe: not yet shown. Starting from the corrector's quoted precision, the search found the astigmatism A1 to 4 Å but put the defocus C1 32 Å off, because it searched C1 before A1. A third search stage fixes the order and is running (§06).
  • For instruments: in energy spread the UltiMono gets there with margin; the Mono at the 90 meV quoted in use, not at its 0.2 eV specification; a Titan's Wien filter only to the edge, at its best; a cold FEG or unmonochromated gun does not (§07).

Parameters, method and the terms used on this page

microscope300 kV, 80 mrad aperture, the ARM200F's measured aberrations (defocus, C3, B2 and A1 retuned for 80 mrad)
specimen, scanPbTiO3/SrTiO3, 5 unit cells (19.5 Å) with 4 Å of vacuum each side; 20 Å scanned in 0.5 Å steps; detector to 133 mrad
knob 1: focal spreadThe gun's energy spread ΔE passed through the objective lens's chromatic aberration Cc: electrons of different energy focus at different heights, a spread of defocus of 17.4 Å rms per eV·mm at 300 kV. We scan it in Å, so the result holds for any ΔE and Cc.
knob 2: source sizeThe gun's finite emitting area, demagnified onto the specimen: each electron's probe lands slightly shifted. Given as the FWHM of that spread of positions, in Å.
how it is simulated16 copies of the scan, each with its own defocus offset and probe shift drawn from these spreads; the diffraction patterns are averaged. No shot noise anywhere yet; atomic vibration only in §06, B.
reconstructionmultislice ptychography (LSQML), 18 slices through the depth
probe fixedthe reconstruction is given the true probe and may not change it. It then has nowhere to put the beam's partial coherence but the object. A control, not an experiment.
probe modesthe reconstruction describes the beam as a mixture of 7–8 mutually incoherent probes and refines them together with the object, so the partial coherence can go into the beam where it belongs.
strongest modehow coherent a beam is: the share of its intensity in its single most coherent component. 100 % is a perfectly coherent beam.
vacuum / slabhow much false structure the reconstruction puts in the empty vacuum layers, relative to the specimen: 0.31 for a perfect beam, near 1 when the vacuum looks as strong as the specimen.
atomic vibrationfrozen phonons, each element's vibration at a given temperature from a Debye model fitted to its room-temperature value; at 20 K only the zero-point motion is left (Ti about 0.04 Å per axis).
outer searchfinding the probe's aberrations from the data: many short reconstructions on a grid of trial values; the trial that fits the data best gives the answer (§06, C).
how it is judgedthe share of Pb and Ti atoms the finder places as the right element within 0.5 Å of their true depth. "Guessing" is the same found atoms slid to random depths along their columns: about 26 % for Pb, 18 % for Ti. The finder matches each column against templates of a lone Pb and a lone Ti atom, reconstructed under the same conditions as the run. good Pb ≥ 80 % and Ti ≥ 45 % · okay Pb ≥ 50 % · off otherwise

Source size alone

questionHow large can the source be (the gun's emitting area, demagnified onto the specimen)? answer0.1 Å: good. 0.2 Å: okay, Ti lost. 0.4 Å: off. Real effective sources are quoted at 0.24–0.6 Å. how it failsDifferently from focal spread: the vacuum stays clean while the columns lose their atom-by-atom contrast. Ti goes first. 05

Probe modes

questionWith the probe fixed, the beam's partial coherence can only go into the object. If the reconstruction instead refines the beam as 7–8 modes (started from the mixed beam a real gun would give), how much comes back? answerPart of it. At 7.29 Å focal spread, off → okay (Pb 31 → 70 %). The other three points are rerunning after a solver bug fix, below.
7.29 Å: off → okay. Pb 70 % (fixed probe 31 %); the false structure in the vacuum halves and separate Pb sites return down each column; Ti 32 % is short of good. The reconstruction kept the beam mixed rather than collapsing it to one probe (fig_modes). The 0.2 Å source run looks cleaner than its control but has no atom scores yet.
A bug in our solver, found and fixed (2026-10-16). Three of these runs crashed at their start. The code sized each object update by the strongest mode's illumination alone, while the update itself adds every mode, so with a realistic beam (strongest mode 9–43 %) the steps were 2–11 times too large. It now uses all the modes; a one-mode run is unchanged. All four points are rerunning with the fix; until they land, this section shows the earlier runs, labelled "earlier solver". One limit remains: the code also shrinks the object step by the number of modes, so in the same 250 iterations a mode run converges its object less, and can score a little below its fixed-probe control.

The Spectra Mono point: cooling, and finding the probe

questionAt the Spectra Mono's own setting (2.5 Å of focal spread, about 90 meV through Cc ≈ 1.6 mm), does the depth survive (A) the beam's imperfections, (B) the atoms' vibration, cooled to 100 K or 20 K, and (C) a probe found from the data rather than given? answerA: yes, good with a 0.1 Å source, okay with 0.2 Å. B: partly, Pb comes back to okay with probe modes, Ti does not. C: not yet shown; the first search failed on its order and is rerunning.
A, coherence only: good with a 0.1 Å source, okay with 0.2 Å. Probe modes take the 0.2 Å point from off to okay (Pb 45 → 55 %). At 0.1 Å the mode run sits a little below its fixed-probe control (Pb 81 vs 91 %, Ti 47 vs 60 %), most likely because mode runs take smaller object steps (§05), not the physics.
B, cooled: Pb comes back, Ti does not. With phonons the fixed probe loses the depth at both temperatures. Probe modes bring Pb back to okay, 51 % at 100 K and 70 % at 20 K; Ti stays at the guessing level even at 20 K, where the atoms still move by their zero-point motion (about 0.04 Å).

C: finding the probe

In an experiment the probe is not given. The corrector reports the defocus C1, spherical aberration C3 and astigmatism A1 with 95 % intervals. We start the run from values off by errors drawn from those intervals, as an operator would, and an outer search has to find the truth before the reconstruction refines the probe as modes. Higher orders are held at their true values.

The first search failed on its order, not its method. It searched C1 × C3 while A1 was still about 40 Å wrong, and its C1 answer slid to the edge of the grid, 32 Å off (about 5 waves at the aperture edge). It then found A1 to within 4 Å regardless. With C1 that far off, the object moved into the vacuum and the depth was lost. The lesson: A1 can be found with C1 wrong, but C1 cannot be found with A1 wrong. A third stage, C1 × C3 again at the found A1, is running.

Placing the instruments

questionWhich microscopes give a focal spread inside the limit of §02? answerThe UltiMono, yes, with margin. The Mono, yes at the 90 meV Cambridge quotes (if Cc ≤ about 2 mm), but not at its 0.2 eV specification. A Titan's Wien monochromator reaches the band between good and off only at its best (~0.1 eV). A cold FEG or an unmonochromated X-FEG, no. caveatThis is focal spread only. Source size (§03) and ripple are not on this axis, and with real sources the joint result of §04 is the one that counts.
