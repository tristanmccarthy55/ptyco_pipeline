The Coherence Wall

On the ARM200F, realistic physics took away the depth of each atom. So we scanned the beam's coherence to find what a microscope must deliver for depth-resolved multislice ptychography. That points to a Spectra 300 with the X-FEG monochromator. Then we put the physics back at its setting, one piece at a time.

Summary

The story

Where we startedThe previous page: the JEOL ARM200F with its measured aberrations at 80 mrad. With a perfect beam and the probe known, the depth works (Pb 90 %, Ti 58 % of atoms at the right depth). With realistic physics it does not. The ARM's own beam (a cold FEG: 0.3 eV energy spread, 0.4 Å source) drops it to guessing (Pb 23 %, Ti 7 %), and so does room-temperature vibration on its own (Pb 24 %). Shot noise was not the cause.
What we didScanned the beam's coherence: the spread of focus that the energy spread causes, and the source size, each on its own and then together, with the true probe given and no other physics (§02–§05). Both are scanned in Å at the specimen, so the limits hold for any microscope.
What it turned upThe Spectra 300 with the X-FEG Mono (§06), for three reasons. Its monochromator brings the energy spread to about 90 meV in use (as quoted by Cambridge), about 2.5 Å of chromatic focal spread: inside the limit, where the ARM's cold FEG gives 7.3 Å and a Titan's Wien filter 4–12 Å. It is a current, widely installed microscope, not a one-off (where they are: §06). And its corrector corrects A5, which the ARM's does not, so keeping the ARM's aberrations is pessimistic for it. The UltiMono's tiny energy spreads are quoted at 30–60 kV; at 300 kV its quoted figures (110 meV at Stanford, < 0.2 eV at Michigan) are no better than the Mono's 90 meV, so the result holds for either.
ThenWe put the physics back at the Spectra Mono's setting, one piece at a time (§07): the source size (A), atomic vibration cooled to 100 K and 20 K (B; room temperature is already ruled out), a probe found from the data rather than given (C), and next, shot noise (D).
ScoreThe share of Pb and Ti atoms put at the right depth, within 0.5 Å. Pure guessing scores about 26 % for Pb and 18 % for Ti. good Pb ≥ 80 % and Ti ≥ 45 % · okay Pb ≥ 50 % · off below that.
AberrationsThe ARM200F's measured set, retuned for 80 mrad, in every run (no measured Spectra set was found).

In short. {{WALL_LINE}} The Spectra Mono, at about 2.5 Å, is inside. Putting the physics back at its setting: good with a 0.1 Å source, okay with 0.2 Å. With atomic vibration, even cooled to 20 K, only the Pb depths survive. A probe found from the data, and shot noise, are the next two results.

What we found

The scan: what the beam must do (true probe given, no other physics)

  1. Energy spread, seen as a spread of focus: the depth survives up to 3.5 Å rms and is lost by 5 Å. For good, that needs ΔE ≤ 0.14 eV at Cc 1.4 mm (≤ 0.08 eV at 2.4 mm). When it fails, false structure fills the vacuum above and below the specimen (§02).
  2. Source size: good up to 0.1 Å, Ti lost at 0.2 Å, off by 0.4 Å. On the Mono at 80 mrad the gun's source image is 0.1 Å up to about 4–8 pA of probe current, so the source sets the current, and with it the scan time (§06). When it fails, each column smears in depth while the vacuum stays clean (§03).
  3. Both together: worse than either alone. 2.5 Å with a 0.2 Å source is off, though each passed alone (§04).
  4. Probe modes, letting the reconstruction describe the beam as a mixture of 7–8 probes instead of one held fixed, win part of it back: at 7.29 Å, off → okay. A bug in our solver crashed three of these runs; it is fixed and the points are rerunning (§05).

The microscope it points to: in energy spread the Spectra 300 X-FEG Mono is inside at the 90 meV quoted in use (not at its 0.2–0.3 eV specification); the UltiMono about the same at 300 kV (its tens of meV are quoted at 30–60 kV); a Titan's Wien filter only to the edge, at its best; the ARM's cold FEG and unmonochromated guns do not (§06).

The physics put back, at the Spectra Mono's setting (2.5 Å of chromatic focal spread)

  1. Source size (A): good with a 0.1 Å source and probe modes; okay with 0.2 Å, where the fixed probe fails. On the Mono at 80 mrad, 0.1 Å means a probe current of about 4–8 pA or less, 0.2 Å about 15–32 pA (§06, §07).
  2. Atomic vibration (B): with probe modes, Pb comes back to okay (51 % at 100 K, 70 % at 20 K); Ti stays at the guessing level even at 20 K (§07).
  3. Not knowing the probe (C): not yet shown. Starting from the corrector's quoted precision, the search found the astigmatism A1 to 4 Å but put the defocus C1 32 Å off, because it searched C1 before A1. A third search stage fixes the order and is running (§07).
  4. Shot noise (D): next, once C works.

Parameters, method and the terms used on this page

microscope300 kV, 80 mrad aperture, the ARM200F's measured aberrations (defocus, C3, B2 and A1 retuned for 80 mrad)
specimen, scanPbTiO3/SrTiO3, 5 unit cells (19.5 Å) with 4 Å of vacuum each side; 20 Å scanned in 0.5 Å steps; detector to 133 mrad
knob 1: chromatic focal spreadThe spread of focus caused by the gun's energy spread ΔE (narrowed by the monochromator) passing through the lenses' chromatic aberration Cc: electrons of different energy focus at different heights, 17.4 Å rms per eV·mm at 300 kV. Other things spread the focus too (high-tension ripple, lens-current noise); they are not simulated. Scanned in Å, so the result holds for any ΔE and Cc.
knob 2: source sizeThe gun's finite emitting area, demagnified onto the specimen: each electron's probe lands slightly shifted. Given as the FWHM of that spread of positions, in Å. How big it is at the sample depends on the gun's brightness and the probe current (§06).
how it is simulated16 copies of the scan, each with its own defocus offset and probe shift drawn from these spreads; the diffraction patterns are averaged. No shot noise anywhere yet; atomic vibration only in §07, B.
reconstructionmultislice ptychography (LSQML), 18 slices through the depth
probe fixedthe reconstruction is given the true probe and may not change it. It then has nowhere to put the beam's partial coherence but the object. A control, not an experiment.
probe modesthe beam is described as 7–8 mutually incoherent probes, refined together with the object. They start from the true aberrations, made into the mixed state a real gun with that setting would give: no outer search, so this is the probe known but released. The probe update runs from iteration 40 of the first stage and 20 of the second, with the aperture constraint on. Only C (§07) starts from a searched probe.
strongest modehow coherent a beam is: the share of its intensity in its single most coherent component. 100 % is a perfectly coherent beam.
vacuum / slabhow much false structure the reconstruction puts in the empty vacuum layers, relative to the specimen: 0.31 for a perfect beam, near 1 when the vacuum looks as strong as the specimen.
atomic vibrationfrozen phonons, each element's vibration at a given temperature from a Debye model fitted to its room-temperature value; at 20 K only the zero-point motion is left (Ti about 0.04 Å per axis).
outer searchfinding the probe's aberrations from the data: many short reconstructions on a grid of trial values; the trial that fits the data best gives the answer (§07, C).
how it is judgedthe share of Pb and Ti atoms the finder places as the right element within 0.5 Å of their true depth. "Guessing" is the same found atoms slid to random depths along their columns: about 26 % for Pb, 18 % for Ti. The finder matches each column against templates of a lone Pb and a lone Ti atom, reconstructed under the same conditions as the run. good Pb ≥ 80 % and Ti ≥ 45 % · okay Pb ≥ 50 % · off otherwise

Source size alone

QuestionHow large can the source be (the gun's emitting area, demagnified onto the specimen)? Answer0.1 Å: good. 0.2 Å: okay, Ti lost. 0.4 Å: off. For scale: on the Mono at 80 mrad the gun's source image is 0.1 Å at about 4–8 pA and 0.2 Å at 15–32 pA (§06). How it failsDifferently from chromatic focal spread: the vacuum stays clean while the columns lose their atom-by-atom contrast. Ti goes first. Caveat: our simulated source is the kind case (at 0.2 Å, 9 modes hold 99 % of its beam, where a real source needs about 17).

05

Probe modes

QuestionWith the probe fixed, the beam's partial coherence can only go into the object. If the reconstruction instead refines the beam as 7–8 modes (started from the mixed beam a real gun would give), how much comes back? AnswerPart of it. At 7.29 Å chromatic focal spread, off → okay (Pb 31 → 70 %). The other three points are rerunning after a solver bug fix, below.
7.29 Å: off → okay. Pb 70 % (fixed probe 31 %); the false structure in the vacuum halves and separate Pb sites return down each column; Ti 32 % is short of good. The reconstruction kept the beam mixed rather than collapsing it to one probe (fig_modes). The 0.2 Å source run looks cleaner than its control but has no atom scores yet.
A bug in our solver, found and fixed (2026-10-16). Three of these runs crashed at their start. The code sized each object update by the strongest mode's illumination alone, while the update itself adds every mode, so with a realistic beam (strongest mode 9–43 %) the steps were 2–11 times too large. It now uses all the modes; a one-mode run is unchanged. All four points are rerunning with the fix; until they land, this section shows the earlier runs, labelled "earlier solver". One limit remains: the code also shrinks the object step by the number of modes, so in the same 250 iterations a mode run converges its object less, and can score a little below its fixed-probe control.

06

Which microscope: the Spectra 300 X-FEG Mono

QuestionWhich microscopes give a chromatic focal spread inside the limit of §02? AnswerThe Mono, at the 90 meV Cambridge quotes (if Cc ≤ about 2 mm), but not at its 0.2–0.3 eV specification. The UltiMono about the same at 300 kV: its quoted 300 kV figures are 110 meV (Stanford) and < 0.2 eV (Michigan); its tens of meV are quoted at 30–60 kV. A Titan's Wien monochromator reaches the band between good and off only at its best (~0.1 eV). The ARM's cold FEG or an unmonochromated X-FEG, no. Our choiceA monochromated Spectra 300, the X-FEG Mono or the UltiMono, at about 0.1 eV: inside on chromatic focal spread, current and widely installed, and with a corrector that corrects A5, so the ARM aberrations we keep are pessimistic for it. Its weak point is the source: a Schottky X-FEG is less bright than a cold FEG and the monochromator cuts the brightness further, so a small source at the sample costs probe current (below). That is why the source size is the first piece of physics put back (§07, A). CaveatThis axis is the chromatic focal spread only. The source size has its own figure below; ripple is not modelled.

How common each is

The point of the Spectra 300 is that it is not a one-off. Below, the sites we could find with a public page, sorted by the gun each names. In Europe the UltiMono turns up more often than the Mono among the pages that say: Imperial, Barcelona and (by its 60 kV figure) Jülich, against Cambridge's Mono; Stuttgart's is monochromated without saying which. These are small numbers and lower bounds. At 300 kV the two quote similar energy spreads, so for this experiment either will do, and together they make at least five monochromated Spectra 300s in Europe, two of them in the UK.

And the source size at the sample

The gun's source image at the sample is set by its brightness and the probe current: d = (2 / πα) √(I / BrV) (Barth & Kruit 1996). At 80 mrad it is much smaller, for the same current, than at the 25–40 mrad where effective sources are usually measured (Müller et al. 2006). On the Mono at 90 meV the gun's own part stays at 0.1 Å up to about 4–8 pA and at 0.2 Å up to about 15–32 pA. The UltiMono at its quoted 300 kV figure is about the same (0.1 Å up to about 5–10 pA); a narrower slit, 30 meV, would cost about three times the current. At 5 pA, a dose of 1e7 e/Å² over our 20 × 20 Å scan takes about two minutes: a small source costs scan time, which a cold stage's drift has to allow.

07

The physics put back, at the Spectra Mono's setting

QuestionAt the Spectra Mono's setting (2.5 Å of chromatic focal spread, about 90 meV through Cc ≈ 1.6 mm), does the depth survive as the physics is put back one piece at a time: (A) the source size, (B) atomic vibration, cooled to 100 K or 20 K, (C) a probe found from the data rather than given? Shot noise (D) comes next. AnswerA: yes, good with a 0.1 Å source, okay with 0.2 Å. B: partly, Pb comes back to okay with probe modes, Ti does not. C: not yet shown; the first search failed on its order and is rerunning.
A, source size: good at 0.1 Å, okay at 0.2 Å. Probe modes take the 0.2 Å point from off to okay (Pb 45 → 55 %). At 0.1 Å the mode run sits a little below its fixed-probe control (Pb 81 vs 91 %, Ti 47 vs 60 %), most likely because mode runs take smaller object steps (§05), not the physics.
B, cooled: Pb comes back, Ti does not. With phonons the fixed probe loses the depth at both temperatures. Probe modes bring Pb back to okay, 51 % at 100 K and 70 % at 20 K; Ti stays at the guessing level even at 20 K, where the atoms still move by their zero-point motion (about 0.04 Å).

C: finding the probe

In an experiment the probe is not given. The corrector reports the defocus C1, spherical aberration C3 and astigmatism A1 with 95 % intervals. We start the run from values off by errors drawn from those intervals, as an operator would, and an outer search has to find the truth before the reconstruction refines the probe as modes. Higher orders are held at their true values.

The first search failed on its order, not its method. It searched C1 × C3 while A1 was still about 40 Å wrong, and its C1 answer slid to the edge of the grid, 32 Å off (about 5 waves at the aperture edge). It then found A1 to within 4 Å regardless. With C1 that far off, the object moved into the vacuum and the depth was lost. The lesson: A1 can be found with C1 wrong, but C1 cannot be found with A1 wrong. A third stage, C1 × C3 again at the found A1, is running.
