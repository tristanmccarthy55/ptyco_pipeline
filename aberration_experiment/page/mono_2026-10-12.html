The Coherence Wall

How coherent must the beam be for depth-resolved multislice ptychography at 300 kV and 80 mrad? The ARM200F's aberrations stay as they are. We turn up the spread of focus (what an energy spread does through Cc) and the source size, one at a time and then together, with the probe known, and find where the depth goes.

01

Where we are

Depth survives, probe known

{{WALL_LINE}}

With the probe known, no phonons and no noise, the depth survives a spread of focus up to 3.5 Å rms and a source up to 0.1 Å, and is gone by 5 Å and by 0.4 Å. For the energy spread that means a monochromator: the Titan generation's Wien filter reaches the wall only at its best, and the Spectra 300's monochromators sit inside it (§07). Real sources at the specimen are 0.24–0.6 Å, at or past the source wall, so the source size may be the harder limit.

What we know

  • Up to 2.5 Å of focal spread the reconstruction looks like the coherent one. At 3.5 Å the vacuum layers start to pick up lattice, but the atoms still sit in the right place (Pb 84 %). From 5 Å the vacuum fills, the columns become leaning streaks, and the depths fall to the guessing level: the cold FEG's artefact.
  • Source size fails differently. The vacuum stays clean while the columns fade into smooth streaks, as room-temperature phonons did on the ARM page. Ti goes first: 0.2 Å keeps Pb (82 %) but not Ti (39 %).
  • Focal spread costs more depth per unit of lost coherence. A 0.2 Å source leaves a quarter of the beam in its strongest mode and is still okay; a 5 Å spread leaves 42 % and fails. A likely reason: a change of focus is exactly what multislice reads as depth.
  • The cold FEG's failure on the ARM page was both effects at once, each enough on its own.
  • Two assumptions here are kind. The simulated source uses 16 configurations, a more coherent beam than a real source of the same size. And there are no phonons yet: on the ARM page room-temperature phonons alone removed the depth at 80 mrad (phase 2).
02

The setup

The target is a widespread 300 kV probe-corrected microscope with a monochromator, of the Titan Themis generation (2013: CEOS DCOR corrector, X-FEG with a Wien monochromator), with the Spectra 300 (2019) for context. The ARM200F's measured aberrations stay as they are; against a DCOR, which corrects to fifth order, they are the pessimistic case. Rather than the energy spread we scan the spread of focus it causes, in ångströms: the energy spread times Cc, 17.4 Å per eV·mm at 300 kV. Cc is not published for every instrument, so one scan covers them all and §07 places them on it.

03

Focal spread alone

Only the focal spread changes from point to point. Up to 3.5 Å the atoms stay in place. From 3.5 Å the vacuum layers above and below the slab start to carry lattice; at 5 and 7.29 Å they are nearly as strong as the slab itself and each column becomes one leaning streak, so the finder still finds the columns but not the depths along them.

04

Source size alone

The vacuum stays nearly clean while the slab loses its atom-by-atom contrast: the columns become smooth streaks. Ti goes first. At 0.2 Å Pb is still placed (82 %) but Ti falls to 39 %; at 0.4 Å both are at the guessing level and about half the found atoms carry the wrong element.

05

Both at once

A real gun has both. Step 2A puts a 0.1 Å and a 0.2 Å source on the two largest focal spreads that were still good alone, 2.5 and 3.5 Å. If the two costs add, a source that is harmless alone could tip a good focal spread over the wall.

06

Letting the probe carry the coherence

So far the probe is held at the central coherent probe, so the reconstruction can put the partial coherence nowhere but the object. In an experiment the probe is refined from the data with several modes, which can carry an incoherent mix. Step 2B repeats the four points that failed or weakened with the probe released and 7–8 modes.

Earlier mode runs started each extra mode at 2 % of the beam and stayed close to that: _true4, on data whose true state has 13 % in its strongest mode, ended with 84 % there. These runs start instead from the exact mixed state of a real gun with each setting, so the test is whether the reconstruction keeps and uses it.

Running. Four of the twelve legs stopped with a numerical overflow in their first iterations and were resubmitted unchanged on 2026-10-15. The likely cause is in the solver: it sets each step length from the strongest mode alone and divides the object step by the number of modes, a weak footing when the strongest mode holds a quarter of the beam. The same rule slows the object down in every mode run, earlier ones included.

07

Placing the instruments

The measured wall in focal spread, with each instrument class drawn as the range its energy spread and Cc give. The source-size wall is not on this axis; it is discussed below.

{{FRAG_INSTR_TABLE}}
  • Cc. Unpublished for the Spectra 300, so its range runs from the ARM200F's 1.4 mm to the Titan's 2.4 mm. The 2.4 mm is the Titan 80-300 with its CESCOR corrector (Müller et al. 2006); a DCOR Themis may differ.
  • Ripple. High-tension ripple and objective-current noise add focal spread in quadrature; on a monochromated beam they can be as large as the energy-spread part. Every bar is a lower bound.
  • Best figures. 90 meV is Cambridge's best quoted value for the Spectra 300 Mono, not its specification (below 0.2 eV); ~0.1 eV is the Wien filter at its best.
  • Source size. Effective sources at the specimen are 0.24–0.6 Å (CEOS 2006): at or past the source wall of §04. Shrinking the source image costs current roughly as its size squared.
